About Me

I'm Dheeraj — a neuroscience PhD student at NCBS Bengaluru by day, and a chaotic ensemble of interests by night (fitting, given my research is literally about ensembles).

My scientific life revolves around the hippocampus, memory, and the question of how a handful of neurons decide to hold onto something forever. I do patch-clamp recordings, single-cell RNA sequencing, and viral injections — basically, I poke neurons and ask them what they remember.

But a lab is only one room of who I am.

I write poetry — sometimes in Hindi, sometimes in silence. I find philosophy the way most people find traffic: unavoidable and occasionally enlightening. I debate for sport and meaning equally. I'm a beginner on the keyboard and dholak, which means I make confident music that is not yet good. I skate with similar energy.

I grew up on anime and OG cartoons, and I will die defending both as legitimate art forms. I game. I code — not because I have to, but because automating something tedious feels like a small personal victory every time. I build websites that I think are cool. I troubleshoot everything: give me a broken thing, and I'll figure it out.

I have a deep, completely unqualified obsession with history and archaeology. I know almost nothing about ancient civilizations. I would still absolutely show up to help excavate a pyramid if someone asked.

Many personalities, one brain — still figuring out how those two facts coexist. That's the research.
